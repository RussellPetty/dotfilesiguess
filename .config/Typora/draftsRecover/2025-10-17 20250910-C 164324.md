### n-Depth Summary

The presentation, led by Wade, focuses on the "People Pipeline Strategy," a systematic approach to building a referral-based business by  cultivating relationships. Wade emphasizes that business is  fundamentally about people and that one's network is directly tied to  their net worth.

He outlines a structured pipeline for managing contacts, which visualizes  relationships as a pyramid. At the wide base are 1,000 people who simply know you. Above that is the "Target 300," a list of potential referral  partners (primarily realtors) that one is actively pursuing. Near the  top is the "Focus 50," a core group of agents who already know, like,  trust, and regularly refer business.

Wade likens a sustainable business to a three-tiered cake, with the layers being **Realtors, Past Clients,** and **Centers of Influence (COIs)**. He warns that refinances are merely the "icing on the cake" and that  abandoning purchase-focused relationships for a refinance boom can  decimate a business in the long term.

He provides detailed definitions for each group:

- 
- **Focus 50 Agents:** These top partners should be contacted weekly and receive special  attention, such as birthday gifts (while being mindful of RESPA  compliance).
- **Target 300 Agents:** This is a wider group to be nurtured through social media engagement  and consistent follow-up, with the goal of moving them into the Focus  50.
- **Raving Fan Clients:** These are past clients who are strong advocates, have done multiple  transactions, or have referred others. They should receive regular  calls, including on their birthdays, and offers for financial reviews.  Wade notes that over 65% of his own business comes from this group.
- **Centers of Influence (COIs):** These are strategic business partners, not personal VIPs. For Wade,  this list is dominated by title company business development reps,  financial advisors, CPAs, and attorneys.

To manage this pipeline, Wade advocates for a dual-system approach: a primary **CRM** to prevent leads from falling through the cracks and a physical **paper list** (he uses a red folder) for portability and accessibility. He shares a  detailed spreadsheet template for organizing contacts and stresses the  importance of capturing names first and filling in details later.

The core of the strategy is consistent, proactive outreach. This includes  handwritten notes, social media engagement, and most importantly,  dedicated time for phone calls. Wade introduces the concept of a daily **"Power Hour"**—one hour (or 4% of the day) dedicated exclusively to outbound prospecting  without distractions. He argues that this consistent effort builds  compound interest over time, leading to a sustainable and thriving  referral business.
